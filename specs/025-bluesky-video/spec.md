## Context and sources

- **Roadmap**: `.specify/roadmaps/video.md`, entry 7 of 8. Goal: platform requirements shown up front, then video on every provider, a per-target video formatter and a TikTok provider. Entries 1 to 6 are merged: the requirements summary and fit badges (1), video groundwork (2), Instagram video (3), Facebook video (4), Threads video (5) and the per-target video formatter (6). This entry builds on entry 1's per-account requirements summary and fit badges, entry 2's video library (probed facts, poster frames, the shared video validator, videos stored in the public bucket), and entry 6's formatter, which adapts a video to whatever limits a provider declares (cut, crop or pad, resize, frame rate, re-encode or rewrap) and makes a target wait for its adapted copy ("version") before publishing.
- **Current state** (from the roadmap, the docs and the code on `main`):
  - Bluesky publishes text posts and posts with 1 to 4 images. Its steps are: resolve mentions (when the text has any), one image upload to the account's server (PDS) per image, then create the post, the only step that may publish. Each image upload fetches the stored file and sends its bytes; the resulting blob is kept in the step state as plain data. Links, mentions and hashtags become facets. The account is connected with an app password exchanged for session tokens (the password is not kept); the session is refreshed under a lease before publishing.
  - Bluesky declares zero videos per post (018 D4): its summary says "Video: not accepted yet", its fit badge for a video says "will be refused", and a post with a video cannot be scheduled to it. Its post types are text, image and carousel. Its publish limits are about 1,666 posts an hour and 11,666 a day (approximate, shared with other apps).
  - Every provider call in a publish step is bounded by the scheduler's provider time limit (10 seconds by default, at most 20), inside a tick that must stay well under 30 seconds (constitution). The worker ticks every 30 to 60 seconds.
  - The formatter (entry 6) decides, per video and target, *as is*, *adapt* (rewrap or re-encode to the target's declared limits) or *refuse*, and the target is published with the original or the target's version. Bluesky gets this by declaring its video limits (024 D17).
- **Research** (phases cannot fetch the web; these files are the only source of external facts): `docs/research/bluesky-video.md`, read with `docs/research/bluesky.md`. Facts used:
  - **Embed**: `app.bsky.embed.video` with a required `video` blob (`video/mp4` only, at most 300,000,000 bytes) and optional `alt` (plain text, no length limit since `@atproto/api` 0.20.41), `aspectRatio` (`width` and `height`, integers of at least 1, may be approximate; the official tutorial says to send it), `captions` (up to 20 WebVTT files of at most 20,000 bytes) and `presentation` (`default` or `gif`). A post's embed is one of images, video, gallery, external, record or record-with-media, so a post has one video **or** images, never both.
  - **Upload goes to the video service**, `https://video.bsky.app` (DID `did:web:video.bsky.app`), not the PDS. The service processes the file, then stores the result on the user's PDS. `@atproto/api` (Docket has 0.22.0, which includes the video lexicons) does **not** mint the service token or call the video service; Docket must do both.
  - **Service auth token**: requested from the account's PDS with `com.atproto.server.getServiceAuth` (`aud`, `exp` in Unix seconds, default 60 s, at most 1 hour ahead; `lxm` the method; a token without `lxm` is capped at 1 minute). For uploads the tutorial and the official client use `aud = did:web:<host of the user's PDS>` and `lxm = com.atproto.repo.uploadBlob`. For `getUploadLimits` the pattern is `aud = did:web:video.bsky.app`, `lxm = app.bsky.video.getUploadLimits` (**UNVERIFIED**). An app password session may mint these tokens (by the PDS source; **UNVERIFIED end to end**).
  - **Upload, single request**: `app.bsky.video.uploadVideo` with the whole MP4 as the body; documented by the tutorial, not marked deprecated (**UNVERIFIED** whether it is).
  - **Upload, in parts** (lexicons in `@atproto/api` 0.20.40, used by the official client): `startUpload` (exact `sizeBytes`, `mimeType`, optional `name`, advisory `durationMs`, `width`, `height`; returns `jobId`, `partSizeBytes`, `partCount`, `expiresAt`; errors `UnsupportedContentType`, `VideoTooLarge`, `VideoTooLong`, `BadAspectRatio`, `DailyLimitExceeded`, `TooManyOpenUploads`, `UploadForbidden`, `ServiceOverloaded`), `uploadPart` (`jobId`, `partNumber` from 1; the body's length must equal the expected part size exactly; parts are idempotent), `getUploadStatus`, `finishUpload` (idempotent; returns `completedJobId` and `jobStatus`; on deduplication `completedJobId` differs from `jobId` and is the one to poll) and `abortUpload`. Host and auth for these calls: **UNVERIFIED** (assumed the video service and the same service token).
  - **Job status** (`getJobStatus?jobId=…` on the video service): `state` (`JOB_STATE_CREATED`, `ENCODING`, `ENCODED`, `SCANNING`, `SCANNED`, `UPLOADING`, `UPLOADED`, `COMPLETED`, `FAILED`; any other value means still in progress), `progress` (0 to 100), `blob`, `error`, `failureCode` (`validation_failure`, `encoding_failure`, `pds_upload_failure`, `pds_upload_unsupported_blob_size`, `generic_failure`), `message`. Poll until `blob` is present or the state is failed. No poll interval or timeout is published (**UNVERIFIED**; "several seconds, minutes of total budget"). An upload of a video already processed returns the earlier blob, which should be used (exact shape **UNVERIFIED**).
  - **Upload limits** (`getUploadLimits` on the video service, authenticated): `canUpload` (required), `remainingDailyVideos`, `remainingDailyBytes`, `message`, `error`. The official client shows `message`, or "You have temporarily reached the limit for video uploads. Please try again later."
  - **Limits**: 300,000,000 bytes; `video/mp4` for the blob; about **25 videos or 10 GB per day per account** (help page, not a lexicon; live values come from `getUploadLimits`); Bluesky-hosted accounts need a **verified email** to upload video. **Duration is unverified**: the official client allows 10 minutes, the launch page says 60 seconds (stale); the research says to treat **3 minutes as a conservative product limit** and to let `VideoTooLong` or a failed job be authoritative. Codec, resolution and frame-rate requirements are not published (**UNVERIFIED**); the research says to send H.264/AAC MP4.
  - **Design notes in the research**: only creating the post publishes; store the job id, never the service token; a retried upload or job is safe because an unreferenced blob is never shown.
- **Brief and constitution vs this entry**: `docs/build-prompt.md` and the constitution list video as out of scope "until a spec says otherwise". Entries 2 to 6 lifted that for the other providers; this spec lifts it for Bluesky video posts, as the roadmap directs.
- **Request vs research**: none conflict. The request says "upload to video.bsky.app"; the research documents two ways to do that, and this spec uses the one in parts (D3) because the single request cannot carry a large file within one bounded step. The research's "check getUploadLimits" is followed with a fallback, because the token for that call is UNVERIFIED (D5).

## Decisions made while specifying

These are judgement calls. Planning records each one in `docs/decisions.md`.

- **D1 — A Bluesky video post is one video and nothing else.** *What:* a post whose media is exactly one video is published to Bluesky as a post with a video embed and the target's text. Bluesky's post types become text, image, carousel and video. Bluesky declares one video per post and no video with images, so a post with two videos, or a video and an image, is refused for Bluesky before scheduling. No post type choice is declared, so the composer shows none for Bluesky. *Why:* a Bluesky post's embed is one video or images, never both, and the research documents one kind of video post.
- **D2 — Bluesky's declared video limits.** *What:* the MP4 container only; H.264 video; AAC audio, or no audio; at most 300,000,000 bytes (decimal); at most 180 seconds. No minimum duration, width, height, aspect ratio, frame rate or bitrate is declared, and no recommended shape. The formatter therefore rewraps a MOV into MP4, re-encodes a video that is not H.264/AAC, cuts a longer video to the first 3:00 of the person's selection, and fits an oversize file under 300,000,000 bytes by bitrate (024 D5, D6, D14). *Why:* MP4 and the byte limit are in the embed lexicon; 3 minutes is the research's conservative product limit (the client's 10 minutes is unverified as a server limit, and an over-long video would only fail late); H.264/AAC is the research's safe encode where Bluesky publishes nothing. Bounds Bluesky does not publish are not invented, so they refuse nothing. *Reverse:* raise `maxDurationSeconds` to 600 once a live check shows the service accepts 10 minutes; drop the codec lists once Bluesky publishes its accepted codecs.
- **D3 — The video is uploaded in parts, one part per step.** *What:* Docket uses the video service's upload in parts: one step starts the upload (sending the exact size, `video/mp4`, a file name and the advisory duration, width and height), then one step per part sends exactly that part's bytes (the part size and count are the service's own), then one step finishes the upload. Each step reads only its own part from storage and never holds the whole file in memory. A part that times out or meets a server error is sent again in a later tick; parts are idempotent. *Why:* every provider call is bounded by the scheduler's provider time limit (10 seconds by default, 20 at most) inside a short tick, so a single request carrying up to 300 MB cannot finish within one step on an ordinary uplink; sending parts in separate steps keeps every step bounded and makes a large upload resume where it stopped, with no change to the scheduler. The host and auth for these calls are UNVERIFIED (assumed the video service and the upload token of D4) and are an owed live check. *Reverse:* the single-request `uploadVideo` is one step and would replace the start, part and finish steps, but only for files that upload within one step's time limit.
- **D4 — Service tokens are minted per step and never kept.** *What:* each step that calls the video service first asks the account's PDS for a service token for exactly the method it calls, expiring at most 5 minutes after the request. Upload calls (start, part, finish) use the audience the research gives for uploads, the account's own PDS (`did:web:<PDS host>`), with `com.atproto.repo.uploadBlob`; the limits check and the job status reads use the video service (`did:web:video.bsky.app`) with their own method (UNVERIFIED, D5). The PDS host is the one in the account's identity record as Bluesky reports it with the session, falling back to the account's configured server address; plan confirms the mechanism from the installed library's types. A token is sent only to the video service, in the request header, and never appears in step state, attempt logs, summaries, messages or snapshots. *Why:* a short, method-scoped token per step limits what a leaked token could do; the research says not to store it; the PDS host behind `bsky.social` is not the entryway address, and the research names the user's PDS as the audience.
- **D5 — Upload limits are checked before an upload starts.** *What:* before starting an upload, Docket asks the video service for the account's upload limits. If `canUpload` is false, nothing is uploaded and the target waits and checks again an hour later, showing Bluesky's `message` (or "Bluesky's daily video upload limit has been reached for this account"); if uploads are still refused 24 hours after the first refusal, the target fails with that message and nothing published. The remaining daily videos and bytes are recorded in the attempt log. If the limits check itself is refused for any reason other than a rate limit or server error (for example its token is not accepted, since the token's audience is UNVERIFIED), Docket records that the check was skipped and starts the upload, whose own `DailyLimitExceeded` is then authoritative and handled the same way. A server error, rate limit or timeout on the check is retried. *Why:* the request asks for the check; a wrong guess about an unverified token must not stop every Bluesky video; the daily cap resets within a day.
- **D6 — Bluesky's daily video allowance.** *What:* Bluesky declares a creation allowance of 25 per 86,400 seconds, named "Bluesky's daily video upload allowance", reserved by the start-upload step (one unit, and one again on a retry of that step). When it is spent, the engine makes the target wait, with no Bluesky call and no attempt used (G22). The 10 GB daily byte cap is not modelled: 25 videos of at most 300,000,000 bytes is 7.5 GB, so Docket's own uploads cannot reach it. Uploads by other apps on the same account are caught by D5. *Why:* the research gives 25 videos a day; the engine already defers cleanly on a declared allowance, and counting only Docket's uploads is approximate on the safe side, as for Instagram and Facebook.
- **D7 — How the job is polled.** *What:* after the upload finishes, Docket reads the job's status, one read per step: the first at least 30 seconds after the upload finished, then at least a minute apart until 10 minutes after it, then at least 5 minutes apart. The job is done when the status carries a blob, whatever its state is called; a failed state ends it (D8); any other state, including one Docket does not know, means still processing. If no blob has arrived 30 minutes after the upload finished, the target fails with "Bluesky did not finish processing the video within 30 minutes; nothing was published", after at most 16 status reads. When finishing the upload names a different job (deduplication), Docket polls that one. If the finish reply already carries a blob, or the service answers that the video was already processed and returns its blob, Docket uses it and reads no status. Each read records the state and progress in the attempt log. *Why:* the research publishes no interval or timeout and advises seconds between reads and minutes in total; the worker ticks every 30 to 60 seconds, so finer reads would not happen; a 3-minute, 300 MB video should process well within 30 minutes.
- **D8 — Bluesky's refusals are explained in plain words.** *What:* a refusal when starting the upload or a failed job fails the target with nothing published, so it can be retried, with a plain explanation and Bluesky's own code and message (secrets removed): `UnsupportedContentType` (not an MP4 Bluesky accepts), `VideoTooLarge` (over 300 MB), `VideoTooLong` (longer than Bluesky allows; Docket cuts to 3 minutes, so Bluesky's limit is lower than expected), `BadAspectRatio` (a shape Bluesky refuses), `UploadForbidden` (Bluesky does not allow this account to upload video; Bluesky-hosted accounts must verify their email first), and for a failed job `validation_failure` (Bluesky found the file invalid), `encoding_failure` (Bluesky could not process the file's encoding), `pds_upload_failure` (Bluesky's video service could not store the video on the account's server; try again later), `pds_upload_unsupported_blob_size` (the account's server does not accept a file this large; common on self-hosted servers), `generic_failure` (Bluesky could not process the video). `DailyLimitExceeded` waits (D5). `TooManyOpenUploads` and `ServiceOverloaded` are retried later. Any other code or message is shown as Bluesky sent it. *Why:* these codes are the only feedback for limits Bluesky does not publish, and the raw codes alone are not readable.
- **D9 — The video embed.** *What:* the post is created as today (text, facets and resolved mentions), with a video embed carrying the blob exactly as the job returned it, an aspect ratio from the published file's displayed width and height (when both are known), and the video's alt text when it is not empty. No captions and no presentation hint are sent. *Why:* the research gives this embed shape and says to send the aspect ratio; alt text has no limit for video, so Docket's image alt text limit does not apply.
- **D10 — Which file is sent.** *What:* Bluesky receives the file entry 6 planned for it: the stored original when it fits as is, otherwise Bluesky's version, and the target waits for that version exactly as entry 6 makes every target wait (024 D11). Docket checks the file's size against what it is about to declare before starting the upload; a mismatch restarts the upload. *Why:* the formatter is the one place that adapts video; the size sent when starting must match the bytes uploaded exactly.
- **D11 — Outcomes and ambiguity.** *What:* only creating the post may publish. Every earlier step (resolve mentions, limits check, start, each part, finish, every status read) is safe to retry: a timeout, a dropped connection, a rate limit or a server error there is retried and never ambiguous, and a refusal there fails the target with nothing published. An upload whose time limit (`expiresAt`) passes before it is finished restarts from the limits check, at most twice, then fails with "Bluesky's upload expired before it finished; nothing was published". A rejected session is handled as today (refresh, then reconnect). A timeout, dropped connection or unreadable reply after the create request was sent is ambiguous and never retried automatically, as today. *Why:* a missed post beats a duplicate; until the post is created the uploaded video is an unreferenced blob that nobody sees.
- **D12 — Saved state and a post that changes.** *What:* the step state records, besides today's mentions and image blobs, the video's upload: the size and address of the file being uploaded, the job id, the part size and count, how many parts are sent, the upload's expiry, the job to poll and when the upload finished, the number of status reads, the start of a limit wait, the number of restarts, and the finished blob. It never records a token. If the post's media no longer match the saved state (the video swapped, its version rebuilt with a different file, an image added), the target restarts from the first step; an upload left behind expires unused. *Why:* nothing is published before the create request, and the start request declares an exact size.
- **D13 — Requirements summary and fit badges.** *What:* Bluesky's requirements summary shows its video limits from its capabilities (one video, no images with it, MP4, H.264/AAC, up to 300 MB, up to 3:00, longer videos cut) and two notes: about 25 videos a day per account, and that Bluesky-hosted accounts need a verified email to post video. The fit badge for a video says "fits", "will be adapted" with the formatter's steps, or "will be refused" with the reason, from entry 6's plan. *Why:* the summary and badges are derived from capabilities (entry 1); the notes are limits Docket cannot check up front.

## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 - Publish one video to Bluesky (Priority: P1)

A member attaches one ready video to a post, selects a Bluesky account and schedules it (or adds it to the queue). At the scheduled time Docket checks the account may upload video, uploads the file to Bluesky's video service in parts, waits until Bluesky has processed it, and creates the post with the video and the text. The target in Docket shows as published, with a link to the post.

**Why this priority**: it is the first deliverable the roadmap names, and the simplest way to get any video onto Bluesky.

**Independent Test**: with mocked XRPC responses, schedule a post with one 1,080 × 1,920, 30-second H.264/AAC MP4 of 12,000,000 bytes and some text to a Bluesky account; drive the ticks through the limits check, start (three parts), each part, finish, several "processing" reads and a read with the blob, then create; confirm each request (service token method and audience, sizes, part numbers and lengths, job ids), the read pace against the DB clock, the embed in the create request, and that the target ends published with the post's address.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a post with one ready video and text, and a Bluesky target, **When** publishing starts, **Then** Docket requests a service token from the account's server and checks the account's upload limits with the video service, and records the remaining daily videos and bytes.
2. **Given** uploads are allowed, **When** the next step runs, **Then** Docket starts an upload with the file's exact size, `video/mp4`, a file name and the advisory duration, width and height, and saves the job id, part size, part count and expiry.
3. **Given** the service asks for three parts, **When** the next ticks run, **Then** Docket sends parts 1, 2 and 3, one per step, each with exactly that part's bytes, then finishes the upload; each step uses its own fresh token, and no token appears in any state or log.
4. **Given** the job reads "encoding" twice and then carries a blob, **When** the ticks run, **Then** the first read is at least 30 seconds after the upload finished and the later ones about a minute apart, and the next step creates the post.
5. **Given** the post is created, **When** the create request is sent, **Then** it carries the text and facets as today and a video embed with the blob exactly as returned, the aspect ratio 1,080 × 1,920 and the alt text when set; the target ends published with the post's address and link.
6. **Given** the create request times out after it was sent, or its reply cannot be read, **When** the tick ends, **Then** the target is ambiguous and never retried automatically.
7. **Given** the limits check, the start, a part, the finish or a status read times out, drops or gets a server error, **When** the tick ends, **Then** that step is retried later and nothing is published.
8. **Given** a post with one video and no text, or with a mention, **When** it is published, **Then** the post is created with empty text, or with the resolved mention, as for images today.
9. **Given** existing text, single-image and multi-image posts to Bluesky, **When** they are published, **Then** they behave exactly as before this entry (same requests, same outcomes, same messages).

### User Story 2 - A failed or slow Bluesky video ends cleanly (Priority: P1)

When Bluesky refuses a video, fails to process it, or takes too long, the member sees the target fail with a reason they can act on, and nothing is published. Nothing is ever published twice.

**Why this priority**: the request names job polling and job failure explicitly; video fails more often and takes longer than images.

**Independent Test**: with mocked responses and the DB clock, drive a video through each documented start refusal and job failure code, a job still processing at 30 minutes, a deduplicated finish, an already-processed upload, an expired upload, a part that times out, and a worker killed mid-step; confirm each outcome, message and number of reads.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a status read reports a failed job with `encoding_failure`, **When** it is read, **Then** the target fails with "Bluesky could not process the video", the plain explanation of D8 and Bluesky's code and message; nothing was published and Retry starts again.
2. **Given** each other failure code (`validation_failure`, `pds_upload_failure`, `pds_upload_unsupported_blob_size`, `generic_failure`) or an unknown code, **When** it is read, **Then** the target fails with the explanation of D8, or with Bluesky's message as sent, secrets removed.
3. **Given** starting the upload is refused with `VideoTooLong`, `VideoTooLarge`, `UnsupportedContentType`, `BadAspectRatio` or `UploadForbidden`, **When** the reply is read, **Then** the target fails with D8's explanation (for `UploadForbidden`, that Bluesky-hosted accounts must verify their email), and no part is sent.
4. **Given** starting the upload gets `TooManyOpenUploads` or `ServiceOverloaded`, **When** the reply is read, **Then** the step is retried later.
5. **Given** the job still has no blob 30 minutes after the upload finished, **When** it is checked, **Then** the target fails with "Bluesky did not finish processing the video within 30 minutes; nothing was published", after at most 16 status reads.
6. **Given** a job still processing 11 minutes after the upload finished, **When** the ticks run, **Then** reads are now about five minutes apart, not one.
7. **Given** a status read reports a state Docket does not know, or a reply it cannot read, **When** it is read, **Then** the job is treated as still processing and read again later.
8. **Given** finishing the upload returns a different job id, **When** the next read runs, **Then** Docket polls that job, not its own.
9. **Given** the upload's expiry passes before it finished, **When** the next step runs, **Then** the upload restarts from the limits check, at most twice, then fails with "Bluesky's upload expired before it finished; nothing was published".
10. **Given** a part times out, **When** the next tick runs, **Then** the same part is sent again and the upload carries on; no part is skipped.
11. **Given** a worker is killed at any step, **When** the target is picked up again, **Then** it resumes from its saved step; if it was killed during the create request, it is ambiguous.

### User Story 3 - Bluesky's video limits shape the video before it is sent (Priority: P1)

A member attaches a 5-minute MOV recorded on a phone (HEVC) to a post for a Bluesky account and an Instagram account. The composer shows Bluesky's video requirements and says, for Bluesky, "will be adapted: cut to the first 3:00 of your selection, re-encoded to H.264/AAC MP4". At publishing time Bluesky receives the formatter's version; Instagram receives what its own plan says.

**Why this priority**: the request asks for Bluesky's limits in capabilities precisely so the formatter trims and encodes for Bluesky; without it most phone videos would fail at Bluesky.

**Independent Test**: for each declared Bluesky video limit, run the formatter's plan over a video that breaks exactly that limit and confirm the plan adapts it (container, codecs, duration, bytes) or refuses it (two videos, video with images); validate each refusal through the composer check, the scheduling gate and the publish-time re-check with no Bluesky request; with the mock storage, confirm the Bluesky target publishes the version, not the original.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a post with a video and a Bluesky account selected, **When** the composer shows that account, **Then** its summary lists video: one per post, not with images, MP4, H.264 video, AAC or no audio, up to 300 MB, up to 3:00 (longer videos are cut), about 25 videos a day, and that Bluesky-hosted accounts need a verified email; no "Post as" choice is shown.
2. **Given** a 5-minute HEVC MOV, **When** Bluesky's plan is made, **Then** it is "will be adapted" naming the cut to 3:00 and the re-encode, and the version that is built is an H.264/AAC MP4 of at most 180 seconds and at most 300,000,000 bytes.
3. **Given** a 40-second H.264/AAC MOV, **When** Bluesky's plan is made, **Then** it is a rewrap into MP4, with no re-encode.
4. **Given** a 40-second H.264/AAC MP4 under 300 MB, **When** Bluesky's plan is made, **Then** it fits as is, and the stored original is uploaded.
5. **Given** a post with two videos, or one video and one image, and a Bluesky account, **When** the composer checks it, **Then** a blocking issue says Bluesky takes one video and no images with it; the post cannot be scheduled to that account until it changes.
6. **Given** a Bluesky target due while its version is still being built, **When** the tick runs, **Then** it waits ("Preparing video for Bluesky") with no Bluesky call, as entry 6 does for every provider.
7. **Given** a ready video in the media library and a Bluesky account selected, **When** the badge is shown, **Then** it says "fits", "will be adapted" with the steps, or "will be refused" with the reason, from the same plan.

### User Story 4 - Bluesky's daily video limit makes posts wait, not fail at once (Priority: P2)

A busy account has already posted its daily share of videos. A member's next Bluesky video post waits until Bluesky allows uploads again, saying why, instead of failing straight away.

**Why this priority**: the request asks Docket to check the upload limits; without it a busy account's videos fail in bulk.

**Independent Test**: with mocked limits replies and the DB clock, drive a target through `canUpload` false (with and without Bluesky's message), a refusal of the limits check itself, `DailyLimitExceeded` on start, 24 hours of refusals, and the declared 25-a-day allowance spent by Docket's own uploads; confirm the waits, messages, that no upload is started while refused, and the attempt log.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the limits check returns `canUpload` false with a message, **When** it is read, **Then** nothing is uploaded, the target waits about an hour, and its status shows Bluesky's message.
2. **Given** uploads are allowed again an hour later, **When** the check runs again, **Then** the upload starts.
3. **Given** uploads are still refused 24 hours after the first refusal, **When** the check runs, **Then** the target fails with Bluesky's message (or Docket's own) and nothing published.
4. **Given** the limits check is refused as unauthorised or not found, **When** it is read, **Then** the attempt log says the check was skipped and the upload starts.
5. **Given** starting the upload returns `DailyLimitExceeded`, **When** it is read, **Then** the target waits as in scenario 1.
6. **Given** Docket has started 25 Bluesky video uploads for this account in the last 24 hours, **When** another Bluesky video target falls due, **Then** it waits with "Waiting for Bluesky's daily video upload allowance", makes no Bluesky call and uses no attempt.

---

### Edge Cases

- **Exactly at a limit** (180.0 seconds, 300,000,000 bytes): fits; limits are inclusive.
- **A video of exactly one part, or a size that is an exact multiple of the part size**: the part count is the service's own; the last part is whatever remains.
- **The file's stored size differs from what was declared when starting** (a version rebuilt between steps): the upload restarts (D10, D12).
- **A silent video**: accepted; no audio track is added.
- **An unknown frame rate or dimensions**: not refused (Bluesky declares neither); the aspect ratio is left out of the embed when width or height is unknown.
- **A rotated phone video**: the aspect ratio uses the displayed frame, as entry 2 records it.
- **Text with a link on a video post**: the link becomes a facet as today; no link card is added (a post has one embed).
- **A video with alt text longer than Docket's image alt text guidance**: sent in full; video alt text has no limit.
- **A self-hosted server (PDS)**: the service token is requested from it and names it as the audience; a server that refuses large blobs shows the `pds_upload_unsupported_blob_size` explanation.
- **The session expires during a long upload**: the publish-time refresh renews it before the next step, as today; a token request refused as expired is retried after a refresh, never ambiguous.
- **A video already uploaded by an earlier attempt**: Bluesky may return the earlier blob or job; Docket uses it (D7).
- **A post with a video for Bluesky and an account that does not take video** (X): Bluesky takes it; X is refused with "This account does not accept video yet", as today.
- **A post created through the public API or by the generator with one video**: published to Bluesky under the same rules; there is no field to set.
- **A target retried after it failed**: starts again from the first step with a new upload.

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements

**Post types and limits**

- **FR-001**: Bluesky MUST declare `video` among its post types (text, image, carousel, video) and MUST NOT declare a post type choice (D1). No composer, schema or scheduler code specific to Bluesky MAY be added.
- **FR-002**: Bluesky MUST declare video limits: one video per post, no images with a video; the MP4 container; H.264 video; AAC or no audio; at most 300,000,000 bytes; at most 180 seconds (D2). It MUST NOT declare bounds the research does not give (minimum duration, width, height, aspect ratio, frame rate, bitrate, recommended shape).
- **FR-003**: Through those limits alone, entry 6's formatter MUST plan Bluesky's file: rewrap a MOV into MP4, re-encode a video that is not H.264/AAC, cut a longer video to 180 seconds of the person's selection, and fit an oversize file under 300,000,000 bytes; no formatter code specific to Bluesky MAY be added.
- **FR-004**: A post with more than one video, or a video with images, MUST be a blocking issue for Bluesky through the one shared validator used by the composer check, the scheduling gate and the engine's publish-time re-check. Bluesky's text limits, counting rules, image limits and facets MUST NOT change.
- **FR-005**: Bluesky MUST declare a creation allowance of 25 per 86,400 seconds, named "Bluesky's daily video upload allowance", reserved by the start-upload step (D6).

**Publishing a video**

- **FR-006**: A Bluesky target whose content is one video MUST be published through these steps, in order: resolve mentions (only when the text has any, as today), check upload limits, start upload, send part 1 … part N, finish upload, check job (repeated), create post. Only create post MAY be marked as able to publish (D11). Text and image posts MUST keep today's steps.
- **FR-007**: Every step that calls the video service MUST first obtain a service token from the account's server for exactly the method it calls, expiring at most 5 minutes after the request, with the audience of D4, and MUST send it only to the video service at `https://video.bsky.app`. Tokens MUST NOT appear in step state, attempt logs, summaries, messages, errors or test snapshots.
- **FR-008**: The limits check MUST follow D5: `canUpload` false or `DailyLimitExceeded` waits about an hour and checks again, failing 24 hours after the first refusal with Bluesky's message or Docket's default; a refusal of the check itself (other than a rate limit or server error) is recorded as skipped and the upload starts; the remaining daily videos and bytes are recorded in the attempt log.
- **FR-009**: Starting the upload MUST send the file's exact size in bytes, `video/mp4`, a file name, and the advisory duration in milliseconds, width and height when known, and MUST save the job id, part size, part count and expiry. Before starting, Docket MUST check that the stored file's size equals the size it will declare (D10).
- **FR-010**: Each part step MUST send exactly one part, numbered from 1, whose byte length equals the service's part size (the last part: the remainder), read from storage without loading the whole file into memory. A part step that times out, drops or gets a server error MUST be retried with the same part number.
- **FR-011**: Finishing the upload MUST record the job to poll: the `completedJobId` it returns when it differs from the job id (deduplication), otherwise the job id, and the time the upload finished. A blob already present in the finish reply's job status, or in a reply saying the video was already processed (its exact shape is UNVERIFIED), MUST be used without polling.
- **FR-012**: Job status reads MUST follow D7: one read per step through the step machine, the wait expressed as "not before" a time with no sleeping in a tick; the first read at least 30 seconds after the upload finished, then at least a minute apart until 10 minutes after it, then at least 5 minutes apart; a blob in the reply completes the job; a failed state fails the target (FR-014); any other state, an unknown state or an unreadable reply means still processing; no blob 30 minutes after the upload finished MUST fail the target with "Bluesky did not finish processing the video within 30 minutes; nothing was published", after at most 16 reads. Each read MUST record the job id, state, progress and any failure code in the attempt log.
- **FR-013**: The create request MUST carry the text, facets and resolved mentions as today and an `app.bsky.embed.video` embed with the blob exactly as returned by the job, the aspect ratio from the published file's displayed width and height when both are known, and the video's alt text when not empty; it MUST NOT carry captions or a presentation hint (D9).

## Assumptions

- Docket's Bluesky connection, app password exchange and session refresh stay as they are; the research says an app password session may mint the video service token.
- The account's PDS host is available from the identity record Bluesky reports with the session (or the configured server address); plan confirms the field from the installed library's types.
- The video service's part size is small enough that one part sends within the provider time limit from a typical server; if it is not, the part is retried until the attempt cap and the target fails with a message naming the time limit. This is an owed live check (FR-027).
- The stored file (original or version) can be read one byte range at a time from storage.
- The scheduler tick runs every 30 to 60 seconds, so a "30 seconds" or "one minute" read is due within one tick of its time.
- Generated, bulk-created and API-created posts with one video go to Bluesky under the same rules; no field needs to be set.
- No `docker-compose.yml` or `.env.example` change is needed: ffmpeg, the worker's background encodes and the larger upload limits arrived with entries 2 and 6, and the video service address is fixed.
