## Context and sources

- **Current state** (from the roadmap, the docs and the code on `main`):
  - Six providers are registered (mock, Bluesky, Facebook, Instagram, Threads, X). No TikTok code exists. A provider is one folder under `src/providers/<key>/` plus one registry line; the scheduler, services and schema do not change for a provider, and every framework addition so far was a **generic** hook (G1 to G24 in `docs/adding-a-provider.md`), recorded in `docs/decisions.md`.
  - OAuth connect is a connect group (`authorizationUrl`, `exchangeCode`, candidates, a chooser), with a declared environment, setup doc, callback-address requirement (G10: Threads and X refuse `http://` and localhost) and callback hint. The callback is always `<BETTER_AUTH_URL>/connect/callback`. X shows a rotating refresh token kept together with the access token; Bluesky shows a publish-time refresh (`needsRefresh`) under a lease.
  - The only per-target choice a provider can declare today is a post type choice (G19, stored per target). There is **no** way for a provider to declare other per-target posting fields, to fetch live account details for the composer, or to record a consent; TikTok needs all three.
  - Every provider call in a publish step is bounded by the scheduler's provider time limit (10 seconds by default, at most 20) and one step runs per target per tick; the worker ticks every 30 to 60 seconds. Bluesky (025) shows a large upload sent one part per step, read by byte range from the stored file. Facebook (021) shows status checks after the publishing request (G23: such checks can only end published, failed by the platform's own report, or ambiguous). G24 lets a wait carry a reason.
  - The media library accepts videos up to `MEDIA_MAX_VIDEO_MB` (1,024 MiB by default). Media is served from `S3_PUBLIC_BASE_URL`; `docs/storage.md` already recommends a public custom domain (for R2 it is required for real publishing). Docket caps a post at 10 media items.
  - Compose reads provider variables through `env_file: .env`, so a new provider variable needs a `.env.example` entry but no `docker-compose.yml` edit.

(3) Video can use `FILE_UPLOAD`, avoiding URL verification. (4) The Login Kit web redirect URI must be `https` (no `http`, no localhost). (5) A creator-info-driven posting UI is mandatory. (6) Sandbox rules are **UNVERIFIED**.
  - **App.** TikTok for Developers portal; client key and secret; app icon, name, description, Terms of Service URL and Privacy Policy URL, web URL, redirect URIs (absolute, `https`, no query, no fragment, at most 10, each under 512 characters). Products: Login Kit and Content Posting API with **Direct Post** enabled. Sandbox and Production modes; review needs at least one demo video of the full flow (at most 5, 50 MB each), "several days to two weeks". URL properties are verified in Production mode only (whether review must pass first is **UNVERIFIED**).
  - **Scopes.** `user.info.basic` and `video.publish` (comma-separated). Whether `user.info.basic` is needed to post is **UNVERIFIED**.
  - **OAuth (web).** Authorize at `https://www.tiktok.com/v2/auth/authorize/` with `client_key`, `response_type=code`, `scope`, `redirect_uri`, `state`; the callback carries `code`, `scopes`, `state`. PKCE is not required for web (sending it is **UNVERIFIED**); use the client secret. Token endpoint `POST https://open.tiktokapis.com/v2/oauth/token/`, form-encoded (exact Content-Type **UNVERIFIED**), for the code grant and the refresh grant. Access token 86,400 s; refresh token 31,536,000 s; the returned refresh token may differ and must replace the old one. The token response returns `open_id`; its other fields are **UNVERIFIED**. Revoke and the `authorization.removed` webhook exist.
  - **Unaudited and caps.** `SELF_ONLY` only, private accounts only (error `403 unaudited_client_can_only_post_to_private_accounts`), 5 users per 24 h. All clients: a creator cap and about 15 posts per creator per day shared across apps (`spam_risk_too_many_posts`, `reached_active_user_cap`, `spam_risk_user_banned_from_posting`).
  - **Creator info** (`POST /v2/post/publish/creator_info/query/`, 20 requests per minute per token), called before every post attempt: `creator_avatar_url` (2-hour TTL), `creator_username`, `creator_nickname`, `privacy_level_options`, `comment_disabled`, `duet_disabled`, `stitch_disabled`, `max_video_post_duration_sec`. Several errors come back as HTTP 200 with an error code in the body.
  - **Required UX** (audit checklist): show the creator's nickname; fetch the latest creator info on each posting attempt and stop when the creator cannot post or the video is longer than `max_video_post_duration_sec`; a privacy dropdown from `privacy_level_options` with **no default**; branded content cannot be `SELF_ONLY`; Allow Comment, Duet and Stitch with **none on by default**, greyed out when creator info disables them, Comment only for photos; a commercial content disclosure toggle off by default with "Your brand" and "Branded content" checkboxes, at least one required when on; the declaration "By posting, you agree to TikTok's Music Usage Confirmation." (or "… TikTok's Branded Content Policy and Music Usage Confirmation." for branded content; link targets **UNVERIFIED**); a content preview and express consent before any media is sent; "it may take a few minutes for the content to process and be visible"; a title in the UI the user can edit; no watermark. **Whether consent captured at scheduling passes audit is UNVERIFIED and is the biggest product risk.**
  - **Video direct post** (`POST /v2/post/publish/video/init/`, 6 per minute per token): `post_info` (`privacy_level` from the creator's options, `title` up to 2,200 UTF-16 code units, `disable_duet`, `disable_stitch`, `disable_comment`, `video_cover_timestamp_ms`, `brand_content_toggle`, `brand_organic_toggle`, `is_aigc`) and `source_info` (`FILE_UPLOAD` with `video_size`, `chunk_size`, `total_chunk_count`). Reply: `publish_id` (at most 64 characters) and `upload_url` (valid 1 hour). Errors: `invalid_param`, `spam_risk_too_many_posts`, `unaudited_client_can_only_post_to_private_accounts`, `url_ownership_unverified`, `access_token_invalid`, `rate_limit_exceeded`.
  - **FILE_UPLOAD chunks.** Each chunk at least 5 MB and at most 64 MB; the final chunk may be up to 128 MB and absorbs the remainder; a file under 5 MB is one chunk; `total_chunk_count = floor(video_size / chunk_size)`, 1 to 1,000. Chunks are sent **in order** by `PUT <upload_url>` with `Content-Type`, `Content-Length` and `Content-Range: bytes FIRST-LAST/TOTAL`; `206` for an intermediate chunk, `201` when complete, `403` for an expired address. Whether a chunk may be sent again after a timeout is not stated (**UNVERIFIED**).
  - **Video specs.** MP4 (recommended), WebM, MOV; H.264 (recommended), H.265, VP8, VP9; 23 to 60 fps; 360 to 4,096 px each side; at most 4 GB. Duration: "up to 10 minutes via API" (transfer guide) **conflicts** with 300 seconds (Direct Post guide); the creator's `max_video_post_duration_sec` is authoritative. Aspect ratio, bitrate and audio codecs are not documented (**UNVERIFIED**).
  - **Photo direct post** (`POST /v2/post/publish/content/init/`, 6 per minute): `media_type=PHOTO`, `post_mode=DIRECT_POST`, `post_info.title` up to 90 and `post_info.description` up to 4,000 UTF-16 code units, `source_info.source=PULL_FROM_URL` only, `photo_images` up to 35 URLs, `photo_cover_index`. Images JPEG or WebP, "max 1080p", at most 20 MB each (PNG not listed). Privacy and interaction fields are the same names as video (**UNVERIFIED** for photo-specific extras). Pulled URLs must be `https`, must not redirect, and the pull times out an hour after it starts; error `url_ownership_unverified`.
  - **Status** (`POST /v2/post/publish/status/fetch/` with `publish_id`, 30 per minute): `PROCESSING_UPLOAD`, `PROCESSING_DOWNLOAD`, `SEND_TO_USER_INBOX`, `PUBLISH_COMPLETE`, `FAILED`; `fail_reason` (`file_format_check_failed`, `duration_check_failed`, `frame_rate_check_failed`, `picture_size_check_failed`, `internal`, `video_pull_failed`, `photo_pull_failed`, `publish_cancelled`, `auth_removed`, spam-risk variants; full list **UNVERIFIED**); `publicaly_available_post_id` only when the post is public **and** approved, so a `SELF_ONLY` post yields no post id and no public URL. Store `publish_id` as the external id.
  - **Webhooks** exist but need a public HTTPS callback per deployer, and the Content Posting events are **UNVERIFIED**; polling is the dependable path.
  - **Inbox upload** (`video.upload`, draft to the creator's inbox) is a different product with its own cap of 5 pending shares per 24 h; it ends at `SEND_TO_USER_INBOX`, not a publish.

## Decisions made while specifying

These are judgement calls. Planning records each one in `docs/decisions.md`.

- **D1 — One TikTok app per install, unaudited until the operator says otherwise.** *What:* TikTok is an optional provider switched on by two operator variables, `TIKTOK_CLIENT_KEY` and `TIKTOK_CLIENT_SECRET` (both or neither; one without the other is a startup error, as for Meta and X). A third optional variable, `TIKTOK_APP_AUDITED`, defaults to false. While it is false Docket treats the app as **unaudited**: every TikTok post is private (`SELF_ONLY`, "Only me"), and the composer, the account card, the requirements summary and the target's status all say so in plain words (D6). The operator sets it to true only after TikTok has approved their app's audit. *Why:* each deployer registers and audits their own app; nothing in the research lets Docket ask TikTok whether an app is audited, so the operator must say. Defaulting to unaudited means a fresh install never promises a public post TikTok will refuse. *Reverse:* remove the variable and always offer the creator's own privacy options.
- **D2 — Connect with TikTok's web login.** *What:* "Connect with TikTok" is an OAuth connect group using TikTok's web authorization with the client key and secret, requesting `user.info.basic,video.publish`, no PKCE. The callback is `<BETTER_AUTH_URL>/connect/callback`. The group declares the callback-address requirement (G10: `https` and a public host) with the reason "TikTok needs an HTTPS callback address that is not localhost." and a link to the setup doc. The exchange stores the access token, the refresh token and both expiries together (encrypted), identifies the account by `open_id`, and names it from a creator info query made right after the exchange ("nickname (@username)"). A callback whose granted `scopes` lack `video.publish` is refused with "TikTok did not grant permission to post. Connect again and allow posting." There is no paste-token fallback. *Why:* the research documents the web flow and its redirect rules; it documents no user-info endpoint, but creator info returns the nickname and username, which are what the audit says to show. *Reverse:* add PKCE if TikTok later requires it for web (an owed live check).
- **D3 — Generic: per-target posting fields declared by a provider.** *What:* a provider may declare a set of per-target posting fields (pickers, toggles, short text) with labels, help, which post types each applies to, and which must be set before scheduling. The composer shows them under that account's target, from the declaration, with no provider-specific UI code; the values are stored with the target, travel to `validate` and to the step machine, and are checked by the provider's own rules through the one shared validator (composer check, scheduling gate, publish-time re-check). A provider that declares none sees no change. *Why:* TikTok's privacy picker, interaction toggles, commercial content disclosure and photo title are mandatory per post; G19 already proved the pattern for one field. *Reverse:* drop the declaration; stored values are ignored.
- **D4 — Generic: live account details for the composer.** *What:* a provider may expose a server-side read of live account details used to build its posting fields (for TikTok: creator info). The composer asks the server, never the platform; the server reads the account's credentials, refreshes them first when they are expired or about to be (through the one refresh path the engine uses, so two refreshes cannot race and a rotated refresh token is never lost), and returns only non-secret details. A recent answer (under one minute old) for the same account is reused, so typing or reopening does not spend TikTok's 20-per-minute budget. If the read fails, the composer says so with a Retry and the target cannot be scheduled until it succeeds. *Why:* TikTok's options differ per creator and change over time; the audit says to fetch them on each posting attempt. *Reverse:* providers that do not declare the read are unaffected.
- **D5 — Generic: explicit consent recorded when a target is scheduled.** *What:* a provider may require consent per target. For TikTok, the composer shows, beside the content preview and the TikTok fields, the declaration text and an unticked "I agree" checkbox; scheduling, queueing or approving a post with a TikTok target is refused until it is ticked. Docket records who consented, when, and a fingerprint of what they consented to (the target's text, media, and TikTok field values, plus the creator details they were shown). Any later change to any of those clears the consent; the post then cannot stay scheduled for TikTok until someone consents again. A duplicated post does not inherit consent. *Why:* the request requires consent captured explicitly at scheduling. **Risk (UNVERIFIED):** TikTok asks for express consent before content is sent; whether consent given at scheduling, for a later automated send, passes TikTok's audit is not known. The risk is recorded in the setup doc, `docs/feature-map.md` and `docs/decisions.md`; if TikTok refuses it, a confirm-at-send flow would be needed, which no spec owns (FR-042). *Reverse:* remove the requirement; consent records are kept but unused.
- **D6 — Privacy: the creator's options, no default, and private-only while unaudited.** *What:* in an audited install the privacy picker lists exactly the creator's `privacy_level_options` in plain words ("Everyone", "Friends" (mutual followers), "Followers", "Only me"), with nothing selected; the person must choose. While branded content is on, "Only me" is disabled with "Branded content can't be private." In an unaudited install (D1) the picker is replaced by a fixed "Only me (private)" value with the explanation "Your TikTok app hasn't passed TikTok's audit, so every post is private: only the account owner can see it. The TikTok account itself must also be set to private." and a link to the setup doc; "Branded content" is disabled (it cannot be private) and "Your brand" stays available. If the creator's options do not include `SELF_ONLY`, an unaudited install cannot post to that account and the composer says so. The target's status everywhere it is shown (composer, post list, post detail, calendar item) carries "Private on TikTok" for a `SELF_ONLY` target. *Why:* the request requires unaudited posts forced to `SELF_ONLY` and surfaced clearly, and the audit requires no default. *Reverse:* none needed; the audited path is unchanged by the flag.
- **D7 — Interactions, disclosure and title.** *What:* video targets show "Allow comments", "Allow duets" and "Allow stitches"; photo targets show only "Allow comments". All start off. A toggle the creator has disabled is shown disabled with "Turned off in this TikTok account's settings." The commercial content disclosure toggle starts off; when on, "Your brand" and "Branded content" checkboxes appear and at least one must be ticked before the target can be scheduled. The declaration text changes with them as the research gives it (shown as text; the policy links are **NEEDS RESEARCH**, so they are left out until the research records their addresses). The post's text (or the target's override) is the caption: the video `title`, or the photo `description`. A photo target also has an optional "Photo title" field of at most 90 characters (counted as TikTok counts). AI-generated-content labelling, cover frame choice and music options are not offered. *Why:* the research's audit checklist; the research gives no requirement for `is_aigc` or a cover in the UI. *Reverse:* add fields to the declaration (D3).
- **D8 — TikTok's declared limits.** *What:* post types image, carousel and video; media required; no text-only posts. Text: at most 2,200 units counted as UTF-16 code units (a custom counting rule, G9), for both video and photo posts (the stricter of the video title and the photo description limits). Images: 1 to 35 (Docket's 10-item post cap still applies), JPEG or WebP (other types converted to JPEG), at most 20,000,000 bytes each, at most 1,080 px wide and 1,920 px tall ("max 1080p" read conservatively, **UNVERIFIED** interpretation). Video: one per post, never with images; MP4 or MOV; H.264, H.265, VP8 or VP9; 23 to 60 fps; 360 to 4,096 px each side; at most 4,000,000,000 bytes; at most 300 seconds. No aspect range, bitrate, audio codec or recommended shape is declared (not in the research). The formatter (entry 6) therefore adapts a video to these limits through the declaration alone. On top of that, the creator's own `max_video_post_duration_sec` is checked: against the value the person saw when consenting (scheduling gate) and against the live value at publish (D10). A publish limit of 15 posts per 86,400 seconds per account is declared, marked approximate and shared with other apps. *Why:* research values; 300 s is the lower of the two conflicting published maxima, and the creator's value is authoritative. *Reverse:* raise the duration to 600 s if a live check shows the Direct Post guide is stale.
- **D9 — Video is sent by chunked upload, one chunk per step, in order.** *What:* a video target runs: check creator (D10), start (`init` with `FILE_UPLOAD`, the exact size, chunk size and chunk count), then one step per chunk, in order, each reading only its byte range from the stored file and sending it with its `Content-Range`, then status checks (D11). The chunk size is chosen per file so the upload finishes well inside the upload address's 1-hour life at one chunk per tick: the file size divided by 30, rounded down, but never under 5,242,880 bytes (5 MiB, which satisfies "5 MB" read either way) nor over 64,000,000 bytes (64 MB read either way); the chunk count is the file size divided by the chunk size, rounded down, as TikTok defines it; the final chunk absorbs the remainder, so it is the chunk size plus fewer than 30 bytes, or, when a bound applies, less than twice the chunk size (always under 128,000,000 bytes); a file smaller than 5,242,880 bytes is one chunk of its whole size. Rounding down keeps the final chunk about the same size as the others, so it fits the same step time limit. Starting is not a publishing request (nothing exists to publish yet); intermediate chunks are not publishing requests; **only the final chunk may publish**. A chunk that times out, drops or meets a server error before the final one is sent again in a later tick; if TikTok refuses the repeat, or the upload address expires (403) before the last chunk, the upload restarts from the creator check with a new start, at most twice, then fails with "TikTok's upload expired before it finished; nothing was published. A smaller file, a faster connection or a longer provider time limit helps." *Why:* the request and research choose `FILE_UPLOAD` for video (no domain verification); every provider call is bounded by the step time limit, so the Bluesky pattern applies; 30 chunks at one per 30 to 60-second tick fit inside an hour. A 1 GiB file means 30 chunks of about 35.8 MB, which needs roughly 30 Mbit/s of upload at the default 10-second limit; this is an owed live check. *Reverse:* a fixed chunk size is a one-constant change.
- **D10 — Creator info is re-checked at publish; a changed answer fails clearly.** *What:* the first step of every TikTok target reads creator info, before any media is sent. The target fails at once with nothing posted, and a plain message, when: the chosen privacy is not in the creator's options now ("TikTok no longer allows '<privacy>' for <nickname>; nothing was posted. Choose again and reschedule."); an interaction the person allowed is now disabled by the creator ("<nickname> has turned off <comments/duets/stitches> on TikTok; nothing was posted."); the video (as it will be sent) is longer than the creator's maximum now; an unaudited install's account cannot post `SELF_ONLY`; or the creator is banned from posting (`spam_risk_user_banned_from_posting`). When the creator has reached a posting cap (`spam_risk_too_many_posts`, `reached_active_user_cap`), nothing is sent and the target waits, checking again about an hour later, showing "TikTok's daily posting limit has been reached for <nickname>; Docket will try again." (G24), and fails with that message if the cap still applies 24 hours after the first refusal. A changed nickname or username is not a failure; the attempt log records the nickname shown. *Why:* the request says a changed privacy must fail clearly instead of posting; the same rule applied to interactions means a post never goes out with settings the person did not agree to. *Reverse:* none needed.
- **D11 — Status checks after the publishing request.** *What:* after the final chunk (video) or after the photo `init` (photo), Docket reads the post's status by `publish_id`, one read per step, declared after-publish (G23): the first at least 15 seconds after the publishing request, then at least a minute apart until 10 minutes after it, then at least 5 minutes apart. `PUBLISH_COMPLETE` publishes the target with `publish_id` as its external id and no link: TikTok returns a public post id only for a public, approved post, and no TikTok link format is in the research (NEEDS RESEARCH), so Docket records that id in the attempt log and builds no link. `FAILED` fails the target with TikTok's reason explained (D13). `PROCESSING_UPLOAD` and `PROCESSING_DOWNLOAD` keep waiting. `SEND_TO_USER_INBOX`, or any status Docket does not know, ends the target ambiguous with "TikTok did not report this as posted; check TikTok before retrying." If nothing final has arrived 60 minutes after the publishing request, the target is ambiguous with "TikTok did not confirm the post within 60 minutes; it may be live. Check TikTok before retrying." While waiting, the target shows "TikTok is processing the post; it may take a few minutes to be visible." *Why:* `publish_id` makes a status read safe; the research says polling is dependable and 30 reads a minute is ample; the worker ticks every 30 to 60 seconds. *Reverse:* webhooks could replace polling (unowned).
- **D12 — Outcomes and ambiguity.** *What:* the creator check, the video `init` and every chunk but the last are safe to retry: a timeout, dropped connection, rate limit (`429 rate_limit_exceeded`: wait at least 60 seconds, or `Retry-After`) or server error there is retried and never ambiguous, and a refusal there fails the target with nothing posted. The final chunk and the photo `init` may publish: a refusal (4xx with a TikTok error code) fails the target with nothing posted; a timeout, dropped connection or unreadable reply after the final chunk was sent goes on to the status checks (the `publish_id` is already known), which can only end published, failed by TikTok's own report, or ambiguous; a timeout, dropped connection or unreadable reply after the photo `init` was sent is ambiguous at once (no `publish_id` is known). `access_token_invalid` before the publishing request is a credentials-expired retry (refresh, then retry); a refused refresh marks the account for reconnect. A missed post beats a duplicate. *Why:* constitution V and the outcome table in `docs/adding-a-provider.md`.
- **D13 — TikTok's refusals explained in plain words.** *What:* each documented error and fail reason maps to a plain sentence, followed by TikTok's code (and message, secrets removed): `unaudited_client_can_only_post_to_private_accounts` ("TikTok only lets an unaudited app post to a private account. Set the TikTok account to private, or, if your app has passed its audit, check `TIKTOK_APP_AUDITED`."), `url_ownership_unverified` ("TikTok could not confirm you own the address your photos are served from. Verify your media domain in your TikTok app; see the setup doc."), `spam_risk_too_many_posts`, `reached_active_user_cap`, `spam_risk_user_banned_from_posting`, `invalid_param`, `file_format_check_failed`, `duration_check_failed`, `frame_rate_check_failed`, `picture_size_check_failed`, `video_pull_failed`, `photo_pull_failed`, `publish_cancelled`, `auth_removed` (also marks the account for reconnect), `internal`. Any other code is shown as sent. Codes are matched exactly; an HTTP 200 reply whose body carries an error code is treated as that error. *Why:* the research's error lists; plain reasons save the operator a trip to TikTok's docs.
- **D14 — Photo posts pull from the media bucket's public address.** *What:* a photo target sends the public `https` addresses of the image files TikTok will receive (the originals or the planner's versions), in post order, with the first image as cover, as `PULL_FROM_URL`. A photo post to TikTok whose media addresses are not `https` is a blocking issue before scheduling ("TikTok fetches photos only over HTTPS."). Docket cannot tell whether the deployer has verified their media domain with TikTok; the requirements summary and the account card carry the note "Photo posts need your media domain verified in your TikTok app." and an unverified domain fails per D13. Videos never need this. *Why:* the research makes photo posts `PULL_FROM_URL` only; the deployer must serve media from a domain they own (`docs/storage.md` already recommends a custom domain). A Docket route that serves media from Docket's own domain is a possible workaround, not built (FR-041).
- **D15 — Tokens: refresh before they lapse, keep the newest refresh token.** *What:* the access token is renewed when it is within 30 minutes of expiry, both ahead of a publish (`needsRefresh`, under the engine's lease) and by the scheduled refresh for idle accounts. Every successful refresh stores the access token and the refresh token returned together, replacing the old refresh token whenever it differs, with their new expiries (the refresh token's expiry is estimated at 365 days from when it was issued when TikTok does not say). A refusal (invalid or revoked grant) marks the account for reconnect; a server error, rate limit or network failure is transient and retried after five minutes, leaving the account active. The account card shows "Reconnect TikTok before <date>" when the refresh token's (estimated) expiry is within 30 days. *Why:* 24-hour access tokens and rotating 365-day refresh tokens (research); the X provider's pattern.
- **D16 — No live check is owed until the app is audited.** *What:* all TikTok behaviour is "verified with mocks only". The setup doc lists the live checks to run once the operator's app has been audited (FR-037); none is owed before. *Why:* the request; an unaudited app can only make private posts to private accounts, which would not exercise the audited path.

### Edge Cases

- **Two TikTok targets in one post** (two accounts): each has its own fields, consent, creator check and upload; one failing never affects the other.
- **The same video to TikTok and other platforms**: TikTok receives its own planned version (entry 6); other targets are unchanged.
- **A video plus images, or two videos, for TikTok**: a blocking issue before scheduling (one video, never with images).
- **More than 10 photos**: Docket's 10-item post cap applies before TikTok's 35.
- **Creator info's options change between composing and scheduling**: the scheduling gate uses the fingerprinted details the person saw; the live re-check at publish (D10) is what protects the post.
- **The operator switches `TIKTOK_APP_AUDITED` from false to true** after posts were scheduled as private: those targets keep `SELF_ONLY` (what the person consented to); new targets get the full picker. **From true to false**: a scheduled target with a privacy other than `SELF_ONLY` fails at publish with "This TikTok app is set as not audited, so it can only post privately; nothing was posted." rather than being silently changed.
- **A post whose video is replaced after the upload started**: the saved state no longer fits the media; the target restarts from the creator check (consent was cleared by the edit, so it cannot be scheduled until consent is given again).
- **The worker is killed mid-upload**: the next tick resumes at the saved chunk if the upload address is still valid, otherwise restarts (D9).
- **An HTTP 200 creator info reply with an error code in the body**: treated as that error, never as success.
- **The creator's avatar address expires** (2-hour TTL): the composer may show a placeholder; the avatar is never stored.
- **A sixth TikTok account posts within 24 hours on an unaudited install**: TikTok's refusal is explained per D13 and waits per D10 when it is a cap; Docket does not count accounts across the install (setup doc explains the limit).
- **Disconnecting a TikTok account**: as for other providers; Docket does not revoke the grant at TikTok (the setup doc says how to remove it in TikTok).
- **A TikTok target without consent reaching publish** (for example, consent cleared by a race with the tick): the publish-time re-check refuses it with nothing posted and "No consent recorded for this TikTok post; nothing was posted."

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements

**Provider, configuration and connect**

- **FR-001**: TikTok MUST be added as one new provider folder plus one registry line; any framework change it needs MUST be one of the generic hooks of FR-012 to FR-014, usable by any provider and inert for providers that do not use it.
- **FR-002**: TikTok MUST be configured by `TIKTOK_CLIENT_KEY` and `TIKTOK_CLIENT_SECRET` (both or neither, startup error naming the missing one, never its value) and the optional `TIKTOK_APP_AUDITED` (default false), all declared by the provider's connect group and documented in `.env.example` (D1). With neither set, TikTok MUST NOT be offered.
- **FR-003**: Connect MUST follow D2: TikTok's web authorization with the client key, `response_type=code`, scopes `user.info.basic,video.publish`, the callback address and the attempt's state; the code exchange with the client key and secret; a refusal when `video.publish` is not granted; one candidate identified by `open_id` and named from creator info.
- **FR-004**: The connect group MUST declare the callback-address requirement (`https`, public host) with its reason and a link to the setup doc, and MUST NOT offer a paste-token fallback.
- **FR-005**: The access token, refresh token and their expiries MUST be stored together, encrypted, and MUST NOT appear in the browser, step state, attempt logs, summaries, messages, errors or test snapshots. The upload address MUST be treated as a secret in the same way.
- **FR-006**: Token renewal MUST follow D15: renew within 30 minutes of the access token's expiry, before a publish and for idle accounts; store the returned refresh token whenever it differs; a refused refresh marks the account for reconnect; a transient failure is retried after five minutes with the account left active; the account card warns 30 days before the refresh token's (estimated) expiry.

**Declared limits**

- **FR-007**: TikTok MUST declare the limits of D8: post types image, carousel and video; media required; no text-only; text at most 2,200 UTF-16 code units through a custom counting rule; 1 to 35 images, JPEG or WebP with JPEG as the conversion target, at most 20,000,000 bytes and 1,080 × 1,920 px each; one video, never with images, MP4 or MOV, H.264, H.265, VP8 or VP9, 23 to 60 fps, 360 to 4,096 px each side, at most 4,000,000,000 bytes and 300 seconds; a publish limit of 15 per 86,400 seconds marked approximate. It MUST NOT declare an aspect range, bitrate, audio codec or recommended shape.
- **FR-008**: Through those limits alone, the image planner (entry 1) and the video formatter (entry 6) MUST plan TikTok's files; no planner or formatter code specific to TikTok MAY be added.
- **FR-009**: The creator's maximum video duration MUST be checked through the shared validator: against the value recorded with the consent at the scheduling gate, and against the live value at publish (D10).
- **FR-010**: A photo post to TikTok whose media addresses are not `https` MUST be a blocking issue (D14).
- **FR-011**: TikTok's requirements summary MUST show its limits from its capabilities and, as notes: private-only, a private account and 5 accounts per 24 hours while unaudited (only on an unaudited install); photo posts need the media domain verified with TikTok; the creator's own maximum video duration applies; about 15 posts per account per day, shared with other apps. Fit badges MUST come from the planners' own decisions.

**Generic hooks**

- **FR-012**: A provider MUST be able to declare per-target posting fields (D3): kind (choice, toggle, short text), label, help, the post types it applies to, whether it is required, options that may come from live account details, and rules for disabling one option based on another. The composer MUST render them from the declaration; values MUST be stored per target, validated by the provider through the one shared validator, and passed to the step machine. Providers that declare none MUST see no change.
- **FR-013**: A provider MUST be able to expose a server-side read of live, non-secret account details for the composer (D4), refreshing credentials first through the engine's single refresh path when needed, reusing an answer under one minute old for the same account, and never sending credentials or raw platform replies to the browser.
- **FR-014**: A provider MUST be able to require explicit consent per target (D5): the composer shows the provider's declaration text and an unticked "I agree"; scheduling, queueing, and approving are refused without it; Docket records the consenting member, the time and a fingerprint of the target's content, field values and the account details shown; any change to them clears the consent; a duplicated post does not copy it; the publish-time re-check refuses a target without valid consent with nothing sent.
- **FR-015**: Each generic hook MUST be recorded in `docs/adding-a-provider.md` (contract table and generic hooks index, next free G numbers) and in `docs/decisions.md` with how to reverse it.

**TikTok's posting fields**

- **FR-016**: TikTok MUST declare: privacy (required, from creator info, no default, D6), "Allow comments" (video and photo), "Allow duets" and "Allow stitches" (video only), all off by default and disabled with a reason when creator info disables them (D7); the commercial content disclosure toggle (off by default) with "Your brand" and "Branded content", at least one required when on; "Branded content" disables "Only me"; an optional "Photo title" (photo only, at most 90 UTF-16 code units).
- **FR-017**: The composer MUST show, for each TikTok target, the creator's nickname, a preview of what TikTok will receive (the target's planned video preview or image thumbnails, and its text), the declaration text matching the disclosure choice, and the consent checkbox.
- **FR-018**: On an unaudited install the privacy MUST be fixed to `SELF_ONLY` and shown as "Only me (private)" with the D6 explanation and setup-doc link; "Branded content" MUST be disabled; an account whose options lack `SELF_ONLY` MUST be reported as unable to post.
- **FR-019**: A TikTok target whose privacy is `SELF_ONLY` MUST be labelled "Private on TikTok" wherever the target's status is shown (composer, post list, post detail, calendar), before and after publishing.

**Publishing**

- **FR-020**: A video target MUST run: check creator, start, chunk 1 … chunk N, status checks (D9, D11). A photo target MUST run: check creator, publish (photo `init`), status checks. Only the final chunk and the photo `init` MAY be marked as able to publish; status checks MUST be marked after-publish (G23).
- **FR-021**: The check-creator step MUST read creator info before any media is sent and MUST fail or wait as D10 says, including the case where `TIKTOK_APP_AUDITED` is false and the target's privacy is not `SELF_ONLY`; it MUST record the nickname in the attempt log.
- **FR-022**: The video start MUST send the `post_info` and `source_info` of User Story 3 scenario 2, with the chunk size and count of D9, and save the `publish_id` and the upload address.
- **FR-023**: Each chunk step MUST send exactly one chunk, in order, with `Content-Type` of the file, `Content-Length` and `Content-Range: bytes FIRST-LAST/TOTAL`, read by byte range from storage without loading the whole file; it MUST accept `206` (intermediate) and `201` (complete) and treat `403` as an expired address (D9).
- **FR-024**: The photo publish MUST send `media_type=PHOTO`, `post_mode=DIRECT_POST`, `PULL_FROM_URL` with the `https` addresses of the files TikTok receives in post order, `photo_cover_index` 0, the description from the text, the photo title when set, the privacy and the comment flag (D14).
- **FR-025**: The privacy sent MUST be `SELF_ONLY` on an unaudited install, otherwise the target's chosen value; `disable_comment`, `disable_duet` and `disable_stitch` MUST be the opposite of each toggle (duet and stitch only for video); `brand_organic_toggle` and `brand_content_toggle` MUST match the disclosure boxes.
- **FR-026**: Status checks MUST follow D11: pace, `PUBLISH_COMPLETE` publishes with `publish_id` as the external id, no link (a returned public post id goes to the attempt log), `FAILED` fails with the explained reason, `SEND_TO_USER_INBOX` or an unknown status is ambiguous, and the 60-minute ceiling is ambiguous; one read per step, waits as "not before" a time, no sleeping in a tick.
- **FR-027**: Outcomes MUST follow D12, and refusals MUST be explained per D13, matching codes exactly, including codes inside HTTP 200 bodies.
- **FR-028**: The saved step state MUST record the step reached, the `publish_id`, the size and address of the file being uploaded, the chunk size and count, chunks sent, when the upload address was issued, restarts, the publishing request's time, status reads and the start of a cap wait, and nothing secret (the upload address is kept out of the state and re-obtained by a restart when lost, or kept encrypted with the credentials; plan decides). State that no longer fits the post's media MUST restart from the creator check; unreadable state MUST never lead to a second publishing request.
- **FR-029**: TikTok MUST publish the files the planners chose for it, and its target MUST wait for a version still being built exactly as every other provider's does.

**Documentation**

- **FR-030**: `docs/limits.md` MUST list TikTok's rows (text length, images, bytes per file, formats, max width, max height, media required, text only, publish limit, videos, video with images, video containers, video codecs, video bytes, max duration, video min/max width and height, min and max frame rate) with source, enforcement point and test, plus note rows for the creator's maximum duration, the unaudited private-only rule and 5-account cap, the photo domain verification, the chunk sizing and the 60-minute status ceiling; the doc-inventory test MUST pass.

### Key Entities

- **TikTok account**: a connected social account identified by the creator's `open_id`, named "nickname (@username)", holding encrypted access and refresh tokens and their expiries.
- **Creator details**: TikTok's live answer for one account (nickname, username, privacy options, which interactions are disabled, maximum video duration); read by the composer (reused for under a minute) and at every publish; never stored beyond the consent fingerprint and the attempt log's nickname.
- **Target posting fields**: per TikTok target, the chosen privacy, the interaction toggles, the disclosure choice and the photo title, declared by the provider and stored with the target.
- **Consent record**: per target, the consenting member, the time, and a fingerprint of the content, the field values and the creator details shown; cleared by any change to them.
- **TikTok publish progress**: the step state of FR-028, keyed by the `publish_id` that becomes the target's external id.

## Assumptions

- The operator registers and owns the TikTok app, supplies Terms of Service and Privacy Policy URLs, and runs Docket at an https public address; until the app is audited, posting is private and limited as the research says.
- TikTok's token reply carries the access token, refresh token, their lifetimes and `open_id` as the research lists; any other field is optional. If the refresh token's expiry is not returned, 365 days from issue is assumed.
- The image planner and video formatter adapt TikTok's files from the declaration alone, as they do for other providers; the formatter's output is MP4 with H.264, which TikTok recommends.
- Stored files can be read one byte range at a time (as Bluesky video does).
- One step per target runs per tick and the worker ticks every 30 to 60 seconds, so 30 chunks finish inside TikTok's 1-hour upload address at typical upload speeds; slower uplinks are covered by the restart rule and an owed live check.
- The composer already shows per-target controls (G19) and per-target previews (entry 6), so the TikTok fields, preview and consent sit in the same place.
- Members who may schedule posts (editor and above) may give TikTok consent; no new role is needed.
- Generated, bulk-created and API-created posts with a TikTok target need a member to complete TikTok's fields and consent in the composer before they can be scheduled for TikTok.
- No `docker-compose.yml` change is needed; the TikTok variables reach the containers through `env_file`.
